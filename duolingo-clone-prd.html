PRD · Interactive Lesson Engine

A five-question Spanish lesson that actually checks your answer.

Beginning learners need a short, clear way to practice a few words and see whether they understood them. A static vocabulary list can't check an answer or show what to correct. This one-week build tests the value of a complete interactive practice loop — a choice, an answer check, and instant feedback — end to end.

01

The problem & opportunity

One focused slice of Duolingo, feasible for three teammates in one week. The chosen slice is the interactive lesson engine; a small Spanish lesson supplies sample content to exercise it. These are project scope decisions — not findings from user interviews or usage data.

The opportunity

Prove the core loop, not a course

Deliver a usable five-question Spanish lesson showing how an exercise engine turns a choice, an answer check, and instant feedback into a complete practice session — validating that a small interactive lesson is understandable and satisfying in a team demo.

Market context

A free demo, not a business

Duolingo is the reference product — freemium, ad-supported, with paid subscriptions. This student clone is a free demo: monetization, ads, and subscriptions are out of scope. Success here means the lesson flow works, not market size or revenue.

03

Proposed solution

A web-based interactive lesson. A learner starts a small Spanish vocabulary lesson, chooses an answer for each question, checks it, and receives immediate correct-or-incorrect feedback. A progress bar advances as questions complete. After the last question, a completion summary appears and offers a replay.

The three MVP value props

Starting and finishing one lesson is the precondition everything else depends on — not a separable value prop, so it isn't listed as one below. The honest Vitamin is the XP reward: genuine delight, not urgent, and the one piece explicitly scoped to be cut first if time runs short.

Acceptance criteria

Exactly five questions, each with one correct answer. The score increments once per correct submission. The progress bar moves from 0/5 to 5/5. The completion screen shows the final score, and replay resets the session. Check mouse, keyboard, and small-screen use.

Dependency & scope rule

The shared question format — prompt, four choices, correct choice, optional explanation — is confirmed on Day 1 so all three workstreams can proceed. If time slips, cut XP and saved progress first; preserve the five-question core loop.

🧭

Open decisions for the team

Choose the frontend framework and hosting on Day 1, based on tools the team already knows. Confirm whether the completion screen shows only a score, or also brief explanations. These do not block the requirements above.
